> Value investing is both an investment philosophy and a way of thinking about business and life. At its core, it's about finding assets trading for less than their intrinsic worth — buying a dollar for sixty cents.

![[chapter-7.webp|Chapter 7 sketch]]
## The Foundation of Value Investing

Value investing rests on three fundamental components:

1. **Base Capital:** The initial amount you invest.
2. **Time Period:** How long you remain invested.
3. **Rate of Compounding:** The annual return on your investment.

## Mastering compound growth

These three elements work together through the magic of compound growth. Understanding their interaction is crucial for building long-term wealth.

## The Rule of 72

This simple formula helps you understand compound growth: divide 72 by your annual return rate to find how many years it takes your money to double.

For example, if you achieve a 12% annual return (the historical average of India's Nifty 50), your money doubles every 6 years (72 ÷ 12 = 6).

## A Practical Example

Consider investing Rs.10,000 at age 25, leaving it untouched until age 75 (50 years), earning 12% annually:

- Time invested: 50 years.
- Number of doubling periods: 50 ÷ 6 = 8.33 (approximately 8).
- Final amount: Rs.10,000 × 2^8 = Rs.25,60,000.

If you increase your return to 15% annually:

- Doubling period: 72 ÷ 15 = 4.8 years.
- Number of doublings in 50 years: 10.
- Final amount: Rs.10,000 × 2^10 = Rs.1,02,40,000.

This demonstrates why even small improvements in return rates create enormous long-term differences.

**Quick Exercise:** Use the Rule of 72 on your own savings—pick an amount, rate (e.g., 10-15%), and time horizon. Calculate doublings: What small change (like starting earlier) could 10x your outcome? This demonstrates why even small improvements in return rates create enormous long-term differences.

## The Evolution of Value Investing

Traditional value investing focused primarily on buying stocks at significant discounts to their calculated intrinsic value. However, the approach has evolved to emphasize:

- **Quality of Business:** Companies with sustainable competitive advantages.
- **Management Quality:** Honest, capable leadership teams.
- **Growth Prospects:** Businesses with expanding opportunities.
- **Financial Strength:** Companies with solid balance sheets.

Modern value investors still seek discounts but are willing to pay fair prices for exceptional businesses.

## Key Principles from Great Investors

### Warren Buffett's Wisdom:
- “Price is what you pay; value is what you get”.
- “It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price”.
- “Our favourite holding period is forever”. 

### Charlie Munger's Insights:
- “It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have got by trying to be consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be intelligent”.
- Focus on avoiding major mistakes rather than making brilliant moves.

## The Importance of Long-Term Thinking

Value investing requires patience. Stock prices can remain disconnected from intrinsic value for extended periods. However, over time, price tends to converge with value.

This long-term perspective offers several advantages:

- Reduces the impact of market volatility.
- Allows compound growth to work its magic.
- Minimizes transaction costs and taxes.
- Reduces emotional decision-making.

## Building Your Investment Framework

To implement value investing principles:

1. **Educate Yourself:** Study great investors, read annual reports, understand basic financial concepts.
2. **Develop Patience:** Accept that good investments take time to pay off.
3. **Think Like a Business Owner:** When you buy a stock, you're buying a piece of a business.
4. **Focus on Quality:** Look for companies with strong competitive positions.
5. **Buy When Others Are Selling:** The best opportunities often arise during market pessimism.

## The Indian Context

In India, only about 3-5% of the population participates in the stock market, compared to over 50% in the United States. This creates both challenges and opportunities:

**Challenges:** Lower financial literacy, cultural preference for real estate and gold.  
**Opportunities:** Less efficient markets may offer better value opportunities.

## Starting Your Journey

Begin with index funds to gain market exposure while learning about individual companies. As your knowledge grows, you can gradually shift toward individual stock selection.

Remember: the goal isn't to get rich quickly but to build wealth steadily over time. Value investing rewards patience, discipline, and continuous learning.
